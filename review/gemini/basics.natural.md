# Naturalness pass (gemini-3-flash-preview) — basics

Overall, the Thai used here is grammatically correct and very common for informal speech between friends. However, since the app is for foreigners/tourists interacting with **strangers** or **shop owners**, the lack of polite particles and certain sentence connectors makes some lines sound blunt or slightly rude.

In Thai culture, speaking to anyone who isn't a close friend without using polite particles (ครับ/ค่ะ) or softening particles (นะ) can make the speaker sound demanding or cold.

| Line | Why a native wouldn't say it | Natural version (Thai + romanization + English) | New words needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| เอาอันไหน | To a shop owner, this is very blunt. It sounds like a command or a cold interrogation. | **เอาอันไหนดีครับ/คะ** (ao an-nǎi dii kráp/kâ) - Which one (should I take/do you want)? | ดี (dii), ครับ/ค่ะ (kráp/kâ) |
| เอากี่อัน | To a shop owner, this sounds too direct. A native would add a polite particle to sound friendly. | **เอากี่อันครับ/คะ** (ao gìi an kráp/kâ) - How many items would you like? | ครับ/ค่ะ (kráp/kâ) |
| ไม่เอาเล็กไป | Two sentences are smashed together without a pause or softening particle. It sounds like a robot. | **ไม่เอาอะ เล็กไป** (mâi ao à, lék pai) - No (I don't want it), it's too small. | อะ (à - softening particle) |
| โรงแรมอยู่ที่ไหน | Asking a stranger for directions this way is quite rude in Bangkok. It needs "excuse me" and a polite particle. | **ขอโทษนะคะ โรงแรมอยู่ที่ไหนคะ** (kɔ̌ɔ-thôot ná kâ, rong-raem yùu thîi-nǎi ká) - Excuse me, where is the hotel? | ขอโทษ (kɔ̌ɔ-thôot), ครับ/ค่ะ (kráp/kâ) |
| ไปนอน | As a statement "I'm going to sleep," this sounds like a command (Go to sleep!). It needs a particle to show intention. | **ไปนอนแล้วนะ** (pai nɔɔn láew ná) - I'm going to sleep now. | แล้ว (láew), นะ (ná) |
| คุณเตี้ยมั้ย | Thais are generally polite about body types. Asking "Are you short?" directly is very awkward/rude unless joking with best friends. | **คุณไม่สูงเหรอ** (khun mâi sǔung rə̌ə) - You aren't tall? (Slightly softer) or just avoid asking. | เหรอ (rə̌ə) |

### General Notes for the App:
1. **Polite Particles (Kráp/Kâ):** For a beginner app, adding **ครับ (kráp)** for male speakers and **ค่ะ (kâ/ká)** for female speakers is the single most important thing to make them sound like "real" people in Bangkok. Without them, a tourist will sound unintentionally aggressive to locals.
2. **"Keu" vs. "Pen":** Your rule for *Keu* vs. *Pen* is excellent—many apps get this wrong.
3. **Pronouns:** Using "Khun" (you) and "Kháo" (he/she) is perfect for beginners. In real life, Thais use nicknames, but "Khun" is the safe, polite standard for foreigners.
