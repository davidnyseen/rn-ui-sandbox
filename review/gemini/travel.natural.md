# Naturalness pass (gemini-3-flash-preview) — travel

Overall, the vocabulary and grammar are quite good for a beginner level. However, some sentences sound a bit blunt or robotic because they lack politeness particles or use "English-style" word order.

In Thai, when talking to strangers (taxi drivers, hotel staff, ticket sellers), it is almost mandatory to end sentences with **ครับ (kráp)** for men or **ค่ะ (khâ)** for women to sound like a "real" person.

Here are the specific lines that sound unnatural:

| Line | Why a native wouldn't say it | Natural version (Thai + romanization + English) | New words needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ไปกรุงเทพฯ ไปรถไฟ | It sounds like two separate sentences: "Go Bangkok. Go train." Thais usually say "Take the train to Bangkok." | **นั่งรถไฟไปกรุงเทพฯ** (nâng rót-fai pai grung-thêep) — "Take the train to Bangkok." | **นั่ง** (nâng = to sit/take a vehicle) |
| ห้องนี้อยู่ที่ไหน | Grammatically correct, but "thîi-nǎi" sounds a bit formal for a room location. We usually just say "yùu nǎi." | **ห้องอยู่ไหน** (hɔ̂ng yùu nǎi) — "Where is the room?" | none |
| ตรงไปถนนนี้ | Sounds like a literal translation of "Go straight this street." A native would say "Go along this street" or "Follow this street." | **ตรงไปตามถนนนี้** (trong pai taam thà-nǒn níi) — "Go straight along this street." | **ตาม** (taam = along/follow) |
| มี ห้องนี้ได้มั้ย | (D1 in Dialogue) It's very blunt. In a service context, the staff would always use a polite particle. | **มีค่ะ ห้องนี้ได้มั้ยคะ** (mii khâ, hɔ̂ng níi dâi mái khá) — "Yes, is this room okay?" | **ครับ/ค่ะ** (polite particles) |
| ได้ ขอกุญแจด้วย | (D2 in Dialogue) Too brief. It sounds like a command. Adding "ครับ/ค่ะ" makes it a request. | **ได้ครับ ขอกุญแจด้วยครับ** (dâi kráp, khɔ̌ɔ gun-jɛɛ dûai kráp) — "Okay, may I have the key, please?" | **ครับ/ค่ะ** (polite particles) |

### Professional Native Tips for your App:

1.  **The "Take" Verb:** You taught "ไป (go) + vehicle," which works, but in real life, Thais use **นั่ง (nâng - to sit/take)** for buses, trains, and taxis 90% of the time. "นั่งรถเมล์" (nâng rót-mee) sounds much more like a local than "ไปรถเมล์."
2.  **Politeness:** I highly recommend adding a "RULE" early in the unit about **ครับ (kráp)** and **ค่ะ (khâ)**. Without them, a tourist using these phrases will sound accidentally rude or demanding to the locals.
3.  **Where? (ไหน vs ที่ไหน):**
    *   **ไปไหน (pai nǎi):** Used for "Where are you going?" (short and common).
    *   **อยู่ที่ไหน (yùu thîi-nǎi):** Used for "Where is [location]?" (more specific).
    *   The app uses these correctly, but locals often shorten "thîi-nǎi" to just "nǎi" in fast speech.
