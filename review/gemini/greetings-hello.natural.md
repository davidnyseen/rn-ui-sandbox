# Naturalness pass (gemini-3-flash-preview) — greetings-hello

Overall, the content is quite good for a beginner. However, in Thai culture, the **polite particle (ครับ/ค่ะ)** is the most important part of "real everyday spoken Thai" when talking to strangers or shop owners. Without them, most of these phrases sound blunt, like a command, or even slightly rude.

Here are the specific lines that need adjustment to sound natural:

| Line (copy the Thai exactly) | Why a native wouldn't say it | Natural version (Thai + romanization + English) | New words needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ไปไหน | To a stranger or shop owner, this is too blunt/direct. It sounds like an interrogation without a polite particle. | **ไปไหนคะ/ครับ** (pai nǎi khá/khráp) - Where are you going? | none |
| ไปเที่ยว | Sounds like a sentence fragment. Natives almost always end a response like this with a polite particle. | **ไปเที่ยวค่ะ/ครับ** (pai thîao khâ/khráp) - I'm going out. | none |
| ไปหาเพื่อน | Sounds like a sentence fragment/blunt. | **ไปหาเพื่อนค่ะ/ครับ** (pai hǎa phɯ̂an khâ/khráp) - I'm going to see a friend. | none |
| ชอบมากค่ะขอบคุณค่ะ | While not "wrong," saying "khâ" twice in such a short sentence is repetitive and unnatural. | **ชอบมากเลยค่ะ ขอบคุณค่ะ** (chɔ̂ɔp mâak loei khâ, khɔ̀ɔp-khun khâ) OR **ชอบมาก ขอบคุณค่ะ** (chɔ̂ɔp mâak, khɔ̀ɔp-khun khâ) | **เลย** (loei) - (emphasis particle, optional but common) |
| ไม่เอาค่ะขอบคุณค่ะ | Same as above; repeating the particle twice in one breath sounds robotic. | **ไม่เอาค่ะ ขอบคุณ** (mâi ao khâ, khɔ̀ɔp-khun) OR **ไม่เป็นไรค่ะ ขอบคุณค่ะ** (mâi-pen-rai khâ, khɔ̀ɔp-khun khâ) | none |
| ไม่เป็นไรค่ะขอบคุณค่ะ | Redundant particles. One "khâ" at the end of the thought is more fluid. | **ไม่เป็นไรค่ะ ขอบคุณ** (mâi-pen-rai khâ, khɔ̀ɔp-khun) | none |
| เข้าใจแล้วก็ชอบมาก | Sounds like a textbook sentence or a robot. It's missing a polite particle and the flow is slightly stiff. | **เข้าใจแล้วก็ชอบมากครับ/ค่ะ** (khâo-jai láew-gɔ̂ɔ chɔ̂ɔp mâak khráp/khâ) | none |

### Additional Notes for the App:
1.  **Pai nǎi maa (ไปไหนมา):** This is the most natural "How are you?" style greeting in Thai. The app uses it well in L2/L3.
2.  **Double Particles:** In Thai, we usually put the polite particle (ครับ/ค่ะ) at the very end of a "speech turn" rather than after every single clause. For example: *"Pai thiao laew-gor glap baan khrap"* sounds much better than *"Pai thiao khrap laew-gor glap baan khrap."*
3.  **Khráp/Khâ/Khá:** Ensure the app distinguishes between **ค่ะ (khâ)** for statements and **คะ (khá)** for questions (high tone). You used it correctly in the RULE, but make sure the audio/choice tiles reflect this distinction.
